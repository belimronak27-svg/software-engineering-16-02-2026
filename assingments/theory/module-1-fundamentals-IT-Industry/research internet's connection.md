# Internet Connection Types: Pros and Cons

## Broadband (Cable/DSL)
**Advantage**
- Widely available in urban and suburban areas
- Affordable and reliable
- Good speeds (25-100 Mbps typical)
- No equipment rental fees usually

**Disadvantage**
- Slower than fiber
- Speed degrades during peak hours
- Limited availability in rural areas

## Fiber Optic
**Advantage**
- Fastest speeds (up to 1 Gbps+)
- Consistent performance
- Low latency
- Future-proof technology

**Disadvantage**
- Limited availability
- Higher installation costs
- Expensive subscription fees

## Satellite Internet
**Advantage**
- Available in remote areas
- No infrastructure needed
- Quick to install

**Disadvantage**
- High latency (poor for gaming/calls)
- Data caps and throttling
- Weather dependent
- Expensive
- Slower speeds (25-100 Mbps)

## 5G/Mobile Hotspot
**Advantage**
- Portable and flexible
- Improving speeds
- Wide coverage

**Disadvantage**
- Data limits common
- Can be unreliable
- More expensive per GB
- Not ideal for heavy usage

## Dial-up (Legacy)
**Advantage**
- Cheapest option
- Widely available historically

**Disadvantage**
- Extremely slow (<1 Mbps)
- Ties up phone line
- Obsolete technology
